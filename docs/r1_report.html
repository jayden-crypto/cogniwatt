A household robot is told “put the mug on the shelf” in a house with several mugs. Resolving the ambiguity costs energy, latency, or the human’s patience. Acting on the wrong mug costs far more — it walks to the wrong room and back.

CogniWatt puts physical actions and cognitive actions in one RL action space, priced against a hardware cost model. The agent learns not just what to do, but how much to think and whether to interrupt the person. This report covers the R1 workstream: the environment, the belief filter, the baselines, PPO, and the results.

The bar is greedy_value — a deliberately strong hand-engineered agent that scores each channel by expected confidence gain per Joule and buys the best one until confident, reserving enough battery to finish. It is what a competent engineer writes without RL.

How it actually went

Four failures, in the order they surfaced — each only visible once the previous was fixed. Three are closed; one is open. All are kept in the report because the diagnoses are the substance of the work.

  1. The agent learned to do nothing

    entropy → 0 · success → 0 · think_tiny × 30 per episode

    Hitting the decision-budget limit cost nothing, so stalling scored −8.24 against −9.52 for attempting the task from the prior. Doing nothing was genuinely optimal. The policy was right; the reward was wrong.

    Fix: non-delivery priced as the worst outcome (−15). For an assistant, unbounded latency is total failure, while a wrong placement is at least recoverable.

  2. Shaping punished being better informed

    plateau at 0.33 — exactly the no-information success rate

    The progress potential measured distance to belief.argmax. Evidence that moved the argmax to a farther candidate produced negative shaping, so the agent was penalised for learning something true.

    Fix: made the potential belief-independent. Success went 0.33 → 0.93 within 400k steps.

  3. Deterministic evaluation created a phantom failure

    held-out 0.700 under argmax vs 0.888 when sampling

    A saturated think_tiny query barely moves the belief, so the observation barely changes, so an argmax policy re-selects the same query forever — up to 10.9 times per episode. The stochasticity is functional: it is what breaks the fixed point.

    Consequence: argmax understated held-out success by up to 19 points, and I initially misread this as a transfer failure. A domain-randomisation “fix” built for it (capability dropout) turned out to add nothing once evaluation was correct — reported here as a negative result rather than quietly dropped.

  4. Training outcome is bimodal — still open

    6 of 10 non-ablation runs converge; 4 collapse to ~0.34

    Collapse is independent of seed and of the question price, and raising the entropy bonus does not rescue it. The uninformed policy is a strong attractor: escaping it requires discovering a multi-step evidence→delivery chain whose payoff is stochastic.

    Next: larger batches to cut gradient variance, or a curriculum that starts with cheap information and raises its price. This is the honest limitation of the current result — the headline numbers are from converged runs.

Two modelling choices worth defending

Evidence saturation

Querying the same model twice does not give two independent observations — a quantised SLM at low temperature is nearly deterministic and its errors are systematically correlated. The n-th query to a tier contributes only γⁿ⁻¹ of an observation.

A cheap model cannot be chained up to the competence of an expensive one. Without this the problem is degenerate — you simply spam whichever channel has the best information-per-Joule. With it, the optimum is a portfolio, which is exactly what both the heuristic and the learned agent discover.

Locomotion dominates compute

At 0.5 m/s and 10 W drive power a grid step costs ~10 J; a 3B inference on Orin Nano costs ~3.6 J. So the energy constraint binds mainly on small-battery NPU-class devices, while latency and patience bind everywhere. The three cost terms are kept separate and swept rather than collapsed into one scalar.

Grounding the simulator in real hardware

With no third teammate, the edge-hardware workstream came back to R1. The rule was simple: whoever runs a model profiles it. Adit captures the RTX 3060 numbers on the passes he already runs for accuracy; the M2 Air anchor, the thermal curve, and the roofline extrapolation to every other device are measured here.

The placeholder costs were optimistic by 10–17×

The original cost table implicitly assumed peak GEMM throughput. A grounding query is a 150-token prompt — a short, skinny matmul that reaches only 19% of the M2's 3.6 TFLOP/s peak (measured). Correcting this moved think_big on the M2 from an assumed 95 ms to a modelled 1591 ms, and on a Pi 5 from 900 ms to 32 seconds — one inference costing more than half of both episode budgets.

Realistic costs made the RL problem genuinely harder — a result in itself

Once a single action could blow half the budget, early exploration generated catastrophic returns and the policy collapsed to guessing — unless pushed much harder to explore. The entropy coefficient had to rise from 0.01 to 0.12, and the runtime now masks any inference the robot cannot afford to finish after (which is what a real scheduler does). Two consequences fell out of the physics, not the tuning:

  • Brute-force local inference stops working. always_big fell from 0.74 to 0.57 success — it now exhausts the battery mid-task.
  • Cloud offload needed a real cost. Priced only on energy and latency, a cloud call strictly dominated on-device inference and the local tiers became dead weight. Home robots don't offload every scene — it leaves the house and it's billed — so a per-call cost restored a genuinely device-dependent decision.
  • The held-out device got honest. The old coral_micro profile was physically impossible (a Coral TPU cannot run a 0.5B LLM at all). It's now a Jetson Nano 2GB, which really does fit the 0.5B model but not the 3B — and the policy gained a memory feature so it can anticipate that from the datasheet.

cogniwatt/interfaces/dialogue.py

Replace four placeholder constants with measurements:

  • TIER_ACCURACY — real grounding accuracy per SLM tier on the referring-expression set
  • EVIDENCE_DECAY — query each model N times on one expression, fit the decay of marginal information gain
  • DEFAULT_ASR_WER — from the Whisper + noise-injection pipeline
  • similarity — embedding cosine, so errors land on confusable distractors

Hard constraint: sample_evidence runs millions of times inside the PPO loop and must never call a language model. Deliver a precomputed lookup, enumerated offline once.

R2 — Adit · hardware half

RTX 3060 compute profile

Captured on the same inference passes that measure accuracy — no separate campaign:

  • Per-call latency and energy above idle for the 0.5B and 3B tiers, median and IQR over 50+ repeats
  • Real wall-clock round-trip latency for the cloud tier, plus its failure rate

One catch worth knowing up front: nvidia-smi samples at ~10 Hz, so a 6 ms call is shorter than one sample period. Loop it 200× and divide, or the numbers are noise.

Where the project stands

  • Done: Tier-1 environment, Bayesian belief filter, task generator with 8 floorplans, frozen VED + dialogue interfaces, 9 scripted baselines, recurrent PPO written from scratch, action masking, potential-based shaping, 12-test suite, Pareto and ablation tooling.
  • Deliberately not used: gymnasium and stable-baselines3. The environment is pure NumPy so it runs bit-identically on arm64 macOS and x86_64 + CUDA, and the ablations need direct access to the hidden state.
  • Open: the bimodal convergence issue; the question-price sweep is only meaningful for λQ ≤ 3.5, since higher prices collapse training.
  • Next for R1: the inherited hardware calibration (M2 Air anchor, thermal fit, roofline extrapolation), then the larger-batch stability study and λQ curriculum, and finally the recurrent-vs-feedforward comparison the belief filter makes interesting — the posterior is an approximately sufficient statistic, so recurrence may buy nothing.
  • Cut, deliberately: the Tier-2 AI2-THOR photoreal port is now a stretch goal. With two people it is the first thing to drop — the novelty claim lives in Tier 1 plus the measured hardware model, and the photoreal port is a demo bonus.
