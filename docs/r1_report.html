Compute cannot buy its way out of referential ambiguity

No model a domestic robot can reach — 0.5B, 3B or 7B, at Q4 or Q8, given unlimited queries or unlimited reasoning tokens — resolves these referring expressions confidently enough to act on. Asking the person does. So with a cooperative human available the decision problem is degenerate: one clarifying question reaches confidence, no model tier can, every agent converges on "ask once" and ties at 0.968, and on-device inference falls to 1 % of evidence purchased.

That is not a failure of the environment — it is the measurement this project was built to make, and it inverts the premise it started from. Dialogue is not the fallback for when compute runs out; compute is the fallback for when dialogue is unavailable.

Which is exactly where the learned policy earns its place. Offline and alone, no single action suffices: the agent must sequence cheap, weak evidence under a budget, and it beats the strong heuristic by 19.1 points of success and the best baseline by +1.75 return.

Training stability — a solved limitation

This report previously carried an open limitation: "6 of 10 non-ablation runs converge; 4 collapse to ~0.34 … raising the entropy bonus does not rescue it." A 48-run sweep shows the diagnosis was backwards. Raising the entropy bonus is the only thing that rescues it.

Convergence is monotone in ent_coef and flat in everything else. Of 48 runs, 9 collapsed and none landed in between — the outcome is genuinely bimodal, so a collapse rate is the right analysis. The collapsed runs are not merely worse: they are the uninformed policy, sitting at the no_think baseline with final policy entropy of 0.001.

The published headline was the lucky seed

The run this report's original numbers came from used ent_coef 0.01, where 1 run in 8 survives. That is why the collapse looked seed-independent and mysterious — at that setting, collapse is the default outcome and convergence the exception.

Two hypotheses, both falsified

Gradient variance. Tripling the batch (16 → 48 envs) changes nothing: 8/8 either way, 0.907 against 0.914. The intuitive explanation is wrong — this is an exploration failure, not an estimation-noise failure.

Recurrence. The GRU ablation had never been run; every number in this project uses the feedforward policy. Measured, it makes no difference — 0.915 against 0.914 — at 3.4× the wall-clock cost (16.2 min against 4.8). This is the direct confirmation of the design claim in agents/ppo.py: the Bayesian belief in the observation is an approximately sufficient statistic, so the POMDP is nearly Markov by construction and there is no residual history for a GRU to exploit. The belief filter does the work recurrence would otherwise have to learn.

Collapse is predictable after 15% of a run

Mean policy entropy over the first 300k steps separates the two modes with no overlap — converged 1.15, collapsed 0.22, Welch t = 18.9. A doomed run is identifiable after about 45 seconds instead of five minutes, so a sweep driver can kill and reseed rather than pay for 2M steps to learn nothing.

Entropy annealing

A single entropy coefficient cannot do both of its jobs. Early it must be large enough to escape the uninformed basin; late it must be small enough for the policy to sharpen. Held constant at 0.12 the converged policy stays deliberately stochastic and over-buys evidence — 4.3 queries per episode against the heuristic's 2.2 — losing on the very objective it optimises.

ent_coefconvergence converged policy
0.011/8sharp, efficient
0.128/8reliable, wasteful
0.12 → 0.01 annealed8/8 reliable and sharp

Annealing keeps perfect convergence, cuts queries 4.4 → 2.1 and energy 180 J → 160 J, and moves return from −1.55 to −0.27. The policy learns to stop buying evidence it does not need — which is the behaviour this project exists to study. PPOConfig.ent_coef_final = None preserves the old constant behaviour exactly, so every earlier run still reproduces.

What a cloud call should cost

The learned policy currently draws 80–95% of its evidence from the cloud. For a project about edge inference whose central claim is a portfolio, that is close to the degeneracy lambda_cloud was introduced to prevent. The fix is not to tune the parameter until the portfolio looks balanced — that would assume the conclusion.

The monetary half is derivable. A grounding query is ~300 in / ~20 out tokens; at Haiku 4.5 pricing that is $4.0e-4 per call. The reward scale is anchored by RewardConfig's own design note — a full 400 J battery costs 10.0 reward, exactly one task failure — so one task ≈ 10 units. Against a domestic assistant doing ~50 tasks/day at a $500/year service value, one task ≈ $0.027, giving ≈ 0.15 reward units per call: an order of magnitude below the 0.5 currently used.

The privacy half is not derivable, and inventing a figure would be an assertion dressed as a derivation. The project already has the right precedent: lambda_ask, the price of the human's patience, is equally underivable and is swept, with the Pareto frontier reported. The same treatment applies here.

When is on-device reasoning rational?

Crossing connectivity with human availability isolates the two alternatives. The hypothesis: the on-device tiers are crowded out by the human, not by a cheap cloud, and local inference only becomes rational when both are gone.

The human is what crowds out on-device reasoning

Removing only the human — cloud still fast and available — more than doubles the on-device share (14% → 33%) and lifts think_big sevenfold. Removing only the cloud barely moves it (14% → 19%) and leaves think_big at zero; the demand goes to ask instead. A clarifying question is such good value that it suppresses local reasoning even when the cloud is unreachable. With neither available, on-device share reaches 63% and think_big rises to 0.94 calls per episode.

This is also a held-out condition that discriminates. On the current device split no baseline drops by more than 0.9 points, so it tests nothing. Offline and alone, always_offload falls 36 points and always_ask 52, while the learned policy keeps a 20-point margin over the best device-blind rule — and its advantage over the strong heuristic is the largest anywhere in the project. The held-out axis should be offline and alone, not a different chip.

Full argument in docs/r1_cloud_price.md. The conclusion reported is deliberately conditional: the agent claim is checked for invariance to the price, while the portfolio claim is a property of the cost model and is stated as "under a cloud price of X, the optimum allocates Y".

Re-verifying the published headline from the saved policy failed at the first step. The checkpoint would not load.

The prose of this report had been updated to discuss the memory feature and the retired Coral profile; its numbers never were. The reward function itself had changed, so the old returns were on a different scale entirely — and the legacy policy made 2.67 cloud calls per episode, precisely the behaviour now priced. greedy_value reads env.cfg at decision time and silently re-optimised for the new reward; a trained network cannot, which accounts for most of the apparent regression.

Fixes. Legacy artefacts are quarantined in results/runs_legacy/ with a provenance note; every ablation and Pareto point was retrained against the current environment; and evaluate_policy.py now refuses to evaluate a policy whose stored RewardConfig differs from the evaluation config. That guard is five lines and would have caught this immediately. This report is now generated (scripts/make_html_report.py) rather than hand-written, so it cannot drift from the code again.

Grounding the simulator in real hardware

The original cost table implicitly assumed peak GEMM throughput. A grounding query is a 150-token prompt — a short, skinny matmul that reaches only 19% of the M2's 3.6 TFLOP/s peak (measured). Correcting this moved think_big on the M2 from an assumed 95 ms to a modelled 1591 ms, and on a Pi 5 from 900 ms to 32 seconds — one inference costing more than half of both episode budgets.

Two things in that table did the most damage to this project's original assumptions. γ falls monotonically with scale (0.87 → 0.64 → 0.44) — the opposite of what was assumed. A more capable model is more self-consistent, so re-querying it buys less; a weak model looks variable because it is nearly random. Ranked by γ alone the 0.5B is the richest evidence source and the worst by every measure that matters, so γ must never be quoted on its own. And the 7B's accuracy is flat in k (0.804 / 0.776 / 0.794) where the small models fall away — adding distractors costs them heavily and costs the 7B nothing, which says its failure is pragmatic inference rather than discrimination.

The ceiling, not the price, is what rules the tiers out

An irreversible place() needs roughly 0.85 posterior confidence to be worth attempting against a ±10 reward. Driving each tier through the belief filter until it saturates:

tierceiling at k=3 reaches 0.85?
think_tiny0.374no
think_big0.546no
offload (7B)0.731no
ask (the human)0.947yes

Two independent implementations of that calculation — one per workstream — agree within 0.010 on every model tier. It is not knife-edge on the 0.85 either: for any threshold between 0.731 and 0.947 the human is the only usable channel, a 22-point window. And the task is demonstrably solvable, because ask clears it comfortably — had it failed too, the right conclusion would have been a broken dataset.

Reasoning tokens do not close the gap. Allowed to think step by step, the 7B gains 0.023 (not distinguishable from zero) at 27× the tokens, and the 3B gets significantly worse (−0.059, McNemar p = 0.016). In 34 % of cases the 7B ignored the instruction and answered directly, which splits the condition for free: 0.811 when it skipped reasoning against 0.801 when it actually reasoned. Quantisation is not the limit either — Q8 scores 0.592 against Q4's 0.603 (p = 0.500) and the two builds agree on 581 of 660 scenarios. Neither better weights nor more thinking moves this tier.

The scenario set was audited by three humans

60 authored cores, blind sheet, no answer key, three independent annotators — one of them outside the project entirely. The gate: a core survives only if every annotator picked the keyed answer. 29 of 60 survive (Fleiss κ 0.592; two-rater sensitivity 39/60 at κ 0.722). Adding the third reader cost 10 cores and 0.13 of κ, which is a finding about item difficulty rather than a reason to drop a rater.

Human-hard and model-hard are different axes

The models are no worse — the 3B is better (0.632 against 0.500) — on the cores humans could not agree about. Model failure therefore does not track item ambiguity: the models fail on expressions three independent people resolve identically. That closes the strongest objection available to this result, which is that the models look bad because the dataset is ambiguous. Measurably, it is not the explanation.

Cost of the audit, stated plainly. Two families are effectively gone — proximity and exclusion retain one core each — and already_satisfied is down to 9 items. Nothing may be cited from any of the three. Two of the three annotators were project members; neither had seen the ground-truth key, and Reader B agreed with it most often of the three (0.840), so the pool is not obviously biased toward the authored answers.

Four silent failures, and what they have in common

The most transferable thing this project produced is not a number. Over its course, four separate settings were found to have silently failed to reach the code that consumed them. None raised an error. All four produced plausible results.

settingwhat happened how it surfaced
lambda_cloudadded to the reward after a policy was trained; the stored config silently took the new defaulta checkpoint refused to load for an unrelated reason, and the reward diff was noticed while investigating
net_qualityconnectivity swept at training time, never passed to the evaluation envscripted baselines returned byte-identical numbers at every link quality — impossible if connectivity were varying
dialoguepolicies trained on measured channel constants, scored in a world still running the placeholdersa spot-check of one agent against a directly computed figure disagreed
scenario idspositional ids assigned after a filter, so every surviving id named a different scenario than the raw table had measured a test written specifically to pin the invariant against the real raw table

Three of the four were caught by a number looking wrong in a specific way rather than by anything breaking — identical values where variation was expected, a disagreement between two routes to the same figure. The fourth was caught by a test that existed only because someone went looking. The common shape is a value that crosses a boundary — training to evaluation, build to analysis, one workstream to the other — with nothing asserting it arrived.

The mitigations now in the code are correspondingly boring: evaluate_policy.py refuses to score a policy whose stored RewardConfig or environment arguments differ from the evaluation's, and refuses to put policies trained in different worlds in one table; r2_build_dataset.py pins id alignment against the real raw table; and both reports are generated from the CSVs, because the earlier hand-written report is how a stale headline survived months of changes to the code beneath it.

Where the project stands

Done

Tier-1 environment, Bayesian belief filter, task generator over 8 floorplans, frozen VED and dialogue interfaces, 9 scripted baselines, PPO written from scratch, action masking, potential-based shaping, hardware calibration on two anchors, training-stability study, entropy annealing, cloud-price derivation, transfer audit, three model tiers measured end to end, a three-annotator human audit of the scenario set, and a retrain on the audited artefact. 31 tests across two suites, 176 training runs.

Stated, not fixed

The ask channel's accuracy is the one constant still assumed rather than measured (0.97, scaled by a measured referent-recovery rate of 0.992). It is the number the degeneracy result leans on hardest, and it is recorded as such in the artefact's ask_provenance. Two scenario families retain a single core each and a third is down to nine items; none may carry a number.

Both workstreams closed

Every dialogue constant is measured — TIER_ACCURACY and EVIDENCE_DECAY from 19,800 queries over three tiers, DEFAULT_ASR_WER from a Whisper pipeline over 721 clips, and the colour-confusability matrix measured and then deleted, because the assumption behind it turned out to be false. The retrain on measured constants is done, on a scenario set three humans independently audited.

Deliberately not used: gymnasium and stable-baselines3. The environment is pure NumPy so it runs bit-identically on arm64 macOS and x86_64 + CUDA, and the ablations need direct access to the hidden state.

Cut, deliberately: the Tier-2 AI2-THOR photoreal port. With two people it is the first thing to drop — the novelty claim lives in Tier 1 plus the measured hardware model, and the photoreal port is a demo bonus.
